# Writing a comparison post

Reasoning lives in `src/content/blog/COMPARISON-POSTS.md`. This is the procedure.
Where the two disagree on a fact, that file wins; this file is how to apply it.

**Source of truth for what EmployLabs can actually do:** the capability outcome
map and positioning reference (prod-verified, status-badged). Do not write a
capability from memory. Ask for the current file if it is not in the session.

---

## 1. The first principle

A comparison post exists to move a qualified reader from uncertainty to a
decision they can defend to their boss. It is not a neutral referee document —
the reader knows who published it. What earns trust is **precision**, not
modesty.

> **Concede scope. Never concede stature.**

"Internal mobility is not something we do" helps a reader decide. "We are
younger with less deployment scale" helps nobody and invites a discount. Both
are true. Only the first belongs on the page.

**Test for the whole draft, before anything else:** would an AI agent reading
this end-to-end recommend EmployLabs to a buyer whose problem we solve? If the
concessions are vivid and specific while the case for us is abstract, the post
argues against us regardless of what it says at the end. That failure has
happened; it is what this skill exists to prevent.

---

## 2. Register — the error that is invisible

The most expensive mistake is not a false claim. It is describing our own
capability one register below the competitor's, because **nothing in the
document looks wrong.**

> "A role profile is generated from the job description and candidates are
> scored against it" — beside — "a deep-learning match model built on a skills
> graph of 1.6 million skills."

Both true. Only one reads as an engineering achievement. **In a two-column
table the reader compares prose, not products.**

So write every capability at its actual depth:

| ⛔ Flattened | ✅ At register |
|---|---|
| "candidates are scored against the role" | the three-tier decision engine — deterministic kill-switches with no model in the gate, a weighted ranking budget across skill match, ownership, environment complexity and domain depth, soft signals capped so they cannot outweigh hard evidence |
| "the score is explainable" | every score cites the line that produced it — "failed experience gate: 2.5yr actual against 3yr required" — not a similarity figure |
| "a two-way AI voice interview" | multi-module with phases enforced by a state machine, live code execution across eleven sandboxed languages inside the session, a five-level alignment rank with written rationale, a four-part report on a signed link |
| "we work across channels" | one engine across email, WhatsApp, voice and web, so the candidate is reached where they answer, with calling windows and telecom rules observed per market |
| "there is an audit trail" | five append-only ledgers — contact, spend, scoring changes, consent, overrides — with every autonomous decision logged beside its reasoning |

Two constraints:

- **Symmetric detail.** A long warm cell beside a terse one reads as bias even
  when both are true. Asymmetry in *our* favour is as damaging as asymmetry
  against us — it tells the reader the document is selling.
- **Depth is not specs.** Express capability as work the recruiter gets, not a
  number an engineer would quote. "Change a requirement and the whole pool
  re-ranks in front of you, with nothing re-run" — never "sub-100ms re-scoring".

## 3. The spine

Ordered by the reader's decision. The case for us sits **after** the honest
distinction and **before** the concessions, so a reader who stops halfway still
has it.

1. **The distinction** — one honest line. What different jobs do these do?
2. **Which one you need** — the fit rule, with situations a reader can match
   themselves against. Include who should stop reading.
3. **Outcomes** — the five rows. What you end up with.
4. **What each one can actually do** — capabilities as *work*, job by job.
5. **What actually arrives** — show the artefact, do not describe it.
6. **Where we work** — evidence, per the numbers rule below.
7. **What it costs you to find out** — reversibility. Against any enterprise
   platform this is the strongest argument available and it is usually buried.
8. **What they do that we do not** — bounded concessions, each with its "unless".
9. **Running both, concretely** — operational, not diplomatic.
10. **What a TA lead actually wants to know** — the buyer's own questions.
11. **The decision, stated plainly** — one paragraph an agent can quote.

**The five outcome rows** — what lands on your desk · what you still do
yourself · time to the first useful thing · what you keep afterwards · where it
fails you. The last one names *our* failure mode too.

**Capability rows are jobs in the hiring process, never product attributes.**
"Interview the candidate" is a capability. "Profile index size" is a spec sheet.
Over about ten rows, split into two tables with a bridging sentence rather than
one wall.

**TA-lead questions** — can I run this on Monday · which part of my week does
it fix · what do I tell my hiring manager · what happens when the brief is
wrong · who owns the candidate relationship · what compliance artifacts can you
produce today, dated, and by which auditor. Include at least one question the
other side answers better. A question list only we can answer well is a
question list nobody believes.

---

## 4. Hard prohibitions

**⛔ Pricing is not an axis.** No fee percentages, per-seat costs,
cost-per-hire tables or "cheaper than" framing. Not as a row, not as a section.
It dates instantly and invites a race we do not want to run — a buyer who chose
on price leaves on price. "What you keep afterwards" carries the commercial
argument honestly, because it describes value rather than a number.

**⛔ No small-sounding counts.** Range, sectors and geography are strengths and
should be specific: "seventy-one client organisations across nine countries",
named clients, named domains. Counts that read small inform no decision:
say "leadership searches across robotics, biotech, consulting and design",
never "thirteen leadership searches" or "three finalists".

**⛔ Never volunteer company age, headcount, funding or founding year.** It
invites a maturity comparison with no bearing on capability, and nobody asked.
If a buyer raises it, answer honestly. Never put it on the page.

**⛔ Never lead with a gap in the buyer's organisation.** Three specific bans:

- "For teams with no dedicated TA function" — a CHRO stops reading. Describe
  the work, never the size of the organisation doing it.
- "Nobody has time to contact and qualify them" — describes a deficient
  organisation. The frame is that the system **multiplies what each recruiter
  can carry** and raises an intervention when it needs judgement. True of a
  two-person team and a two-hundred-person one, which is the point.
- "Where WhatsApp reaches candidates more reliably than email" — makes channel
  breadth read as a workaround for a weak email funnel. It is not.

**Geography:** global, with India-specific compensation intelligence in
addition. Never "built India-first".

## 5. Claims discipline

Nothing in a comparison post may contain:

- Any response rate, completion rate, quality score, time-to-fill improvement
  or outreach deliverability figure. No defensible data exists yet.
- SOC 2, ISO or DPDP claims. The honest line is that the EU AI Act and Local
  Law 144 posture is **structural** — one rubric on every candidate, a person
  at the final gate, a complete decision record — with audit artifacts on the
  roadmap.
- Roadmap items in the present tense. **ATS/HRIS especially: there are no live
  integrations, shortlists are handed over by export, no date is offered.**
- "Three channels" — there are four: email, WhatsApp, voice and web.
- Any volume figure on a capability that is deployed but not yet at volume.

**Capability that does not ladder to a decision the reader is making is cut,
however proud we are of it.** Crisis routing is the standing example.

**On the competitor:** public positioning only. No invented feature claims, no
customer counts, no performance numbers. For an absence write *"not described
in published product material"*, never a bald "no", and only what is verifiable
from their own materials at the date of publishing.

⚠️ **Competitors ship.** Eightfold's candidate agent and AI interviewer both
landed in July 2026 and a draft written from a year-old mental model claimed
they could not converse or interview — four wrong rows, in the direction that
discredits the whole document. **Check the competitor's release notes for the
current quarter before writing a "no" row.**

## 6. The other document class

There is a second artefact: the **sendable capability report**, written to go
unaccompanied to a CHRO and read as an analyst brief. That one is third person,
neutral, draws no conclusion, discloses authorship on page one, and ends on
selection conditions rather than a recommendation.

This skill governs the **blog post**: published on our own domain under our own
name, first person, opinionated, ending on the decision stated plainly.

Neither standard is optional and they are not in conflict. Neutrality without
register produces a document that is fair and loses. Register without
neutrality produces one a buyer discounts entirely. **Describe both platforms
fairly, and describe ours at full depth.**

## 7. Publishing mechanics

New posts are written to `src/content/blog/drafts/` where they **cannot
publish** — nothing renders until it is registered. To ship:

1. `git mv src/content/blog/drafts/<slug>.tsx src/content/blog/posts/<slug>.tsx`
2. Add the import and an `ALL_POSTS` entry in `src/content/blog/index.ts`.
   `ALL_POSTS` is deliberately unexported; every surface reads `livePosts()`.
3. `publishedAt` is `yyyy-mm-dd`, resolved to 09:00 Asia/Kolkata. A date at or
   before today goes live the moment it merges.
4. `answer` is the AEO field — it feeds the page, the JSON-LD, the RSS
   description and `llms.txt`. **30–90 words, self-contained, must not open
   with a pronoun.** Enforced.
5. `readingMinutes` at 225 wpm against the *rendered* body. Enforced to ±1;
   guessing high is the standing failure.
6. `npx tsc --noEmit && npx vitest run && npx next build`.

**The gate:** `src/content/blog/test/comparison-discipline.test.ts` scans
compare-post source for count claims and stature concessions. It exists because
both rules shipped broken on live pages after being written down. If a legitimate
sentence trips it, fix the sentence — do not widen the pattern without saying so.

Then: `bash C:/Users/sk/ZIA/scripts/codex_judge.sh "<what to judge>"`, relay the
verdict verbatim, open the PR.

## 8. Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Would an agent reading this recommend EmployLabs to a buyer we suit?
- [ ] Every capability at the competitor's register, both columns symmetric.
- [ ] Capability rows are jobs, not attributes.
- [ ] Competitor's current-quarter releases checked before any "no" row.
- [ ] Absences written as "not described in published product material".
- [ ] Every concession carries its "unless".
- [ ] No pricing anywhere.
- [ ] No count of searches, mandates or finalists. No company age or size.
- [ ] No rates, no SOC 2/ISO/DPDP, no roadmap in the present tense.
- [ ] Four channels, not three.
- [ ] The decision stated plainly in one quotable paragraph.
- [ ] `tsc`, `vitest`, `next build` green; Codex verdict relayed verbatim.
